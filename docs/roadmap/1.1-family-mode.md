# Family mode (planned for 1.1)

**Update, 2026-10-03:** profiles were built early, at the owner's request, and differently from section 1 below: each profile has **its own library folder** (the owner: "I don't want to mix our music"), not shared files with a filter over them. A kids profile today is just a profile. The parent PIN, the explicit filter and the clean-first rule below are still only a plan, and would now apply to a profile's own library.

**Update, 2026-10-03:** a profile can be marked as **a child's** (a switch when it's made, and in its ⋯ menu; a "Child" badge in the list). The owner: "Child toggle will do certain things that we will discuss later, most likely remove explicit songs, and not allow R18 songs." Until that's decided the mark does nothing at all. Nothing stops the mark being taken off again: that waits for the PIN.

**Status: a plan, not a build step.** Nothing here is built before the Mac app has playlists and profiles. It records what the owner asked for on 2026-09-30.

## What it's for

A child (under 13, say) can use the app to find and play music, and keep their own playlists. They get clean music by default, and a clear message when a song isn't suitable.

## 1. Profiles on this computer, with a parent PIN

- Local profiles only, e.g. "Me" and "Kids". There are no online accounts; "accounts and cloud anything" stays out.
- A parent PIN switches profiles, and locks the settings, downloads and the YouTube search box.
- The music files are shared. A profile only changes what's visible and playable, plus its own playlists.

## 2. Filtering by song, not by artist

- The filter is each track's **explicit** tag. The engine already writes it (contract section 4: `rtng` in M4A, `ITUNESADVISORY` in MP3 and Vorbis), from YouTube Music's `isExplicit`.
- **An artist is never blocked as a whole.** 2Pac has clean songs and radio edits, and those stay available.
- **Clean first.** A kids profile prefers the clean version when both exist, the reverse of the owner's own rule (explicit unless the name says clean, `prefer_explicit` in config.json). The fingerprint gate can't tell a clean edit from the explicit one (step 08's known limit), so this relies on the tag and step 06's clean/explicit rule.
- **Unrated songs wait for a parent.** An only-copy rip has no rating from YouTube Music. In a kids profile it's hidden until a parent approves it; it's never assumed clean.

## 3. The message

- Word it plainly, without claiming an age rating. Music has no official one; the industry label is "Parental Advisory: Explicit Content". For example:

  > This song is marked explicit. Ask a parent to allow it.

- **"Contains …" only when we know.** YouTube Music says explicit yes or no, not why. Step 10 fetches lyrics, so a scan for strong language can honestly say "Contains strong language".
- **Never guess themes** such as violence or drugs from the lyrics. A wrong warning is worse than a plain one.
- A parent can allow a single song from the message (with the PIN). The approval is kept locally, per profile.

## 4. Where it applies

- the library view and the player
- the kids profile's playlists
- Discover (roadmap 0.4): explicit picks are left out, and Discover runs only with a parent's OK
- the search box that plays anything from YouTube: it filters out explicit results, or is locked by the PIN

## 5. YouTube's terms

YouTube's terms require users to be 13 or older; younger children are meant to use YouTube Kids or a supervised account. So in family mode:
- the **parent** sets up the app and runs downloads
- the child plays music and builds playlists
