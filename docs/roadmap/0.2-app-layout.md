## Settings

**General**
- A song downloaded from Discover: does it stay in Discover's Downloads, or go to the main library's Recently Added? Choices: **Discover Downloads** / **All Library**. (This is parked Fix A-2 as a setting.)

**Quality**
- **128 / 256.** Side note shown to the user: "Standard is 128 kbps AAC. YouTube Premium offers 256. Please log in to your YouTube account to use this."
- **Downloads per day: 250** (the owner can type another number). Side note: "The standard download rate is 300 songs per 24 hours. We keep it at 250 on purpose, to avoid trouble with YouTube. You can raise it at your own risk. It can't go above 300."

**Accounts**
- **YouTube**
- **Apple Music** (can only read your music list; the songs are then downloaded slowly from YouTube)
- **Spotify** (the same)

**Lyrics** (owner's idea, 2026-10-01)
- **Find missing lyrics:** one button. It takes every song with no lyrics, looks each up (LRCLIB, then YouTube Music), and saves what it finds, with a progress bar. The engine already does this (`musicorg lyrics --missing`: a plan, journaled, undoable, one lookup a second); the button only starts it and shows how it's going.
- Also suggested for Settings: the library folder, the owner's preferred spellings (like "Jay Z"), and an Activity page with Undo.

## Notes for building it

- "Library +" and removable entries are the sidebar part of parked Fix A-3.
- Discover is v0.4 (`0.4-discover.md`). Until it's built, the YouTube Music search page lives where Discover will be.
- "Not Identified Yet" isn't in the owner's list. Suggestion: it stays as an optional Library entry that hides itself when it's empty.
- The 160 kbps Opus option isn't in the owner's Quality list. It stays parked in `docs/ROADMAP.md` unless the owner asks for it.
- The engine's daily cap is already a setting (`config.json`); its default would change from 300 to 250, with 300 as the most allowed.
- Timed lyrics for words typed by hand (owner asked, 2026-10-01). Three ways, simplest first:
  1. **Look them up:** a "Find timed lyrics" button that searches LRCLIB by the song's title and artist. The engine already does this for identified songs.
  2. **Tap along:** play the song and press a key as each line starts. No new software, and it always works.
  3. **Work it out from the audio:** a program that listens to the song and lines the words up. It needs a large new dependency, is slow on the iMac, and makes mistakes on sung music. Only if 1 and 2 aren't enough; it needs the owner's OK as a new dependency.
- *Built 2026-10-01:* **Lyrics while playing from YouTube Music** (owner, 2026-10-01): a song played from the search page shows no lyrics today. It should look them up the same way (LRCLIB, then YouTube Music), fitted to the song's length, and show them timed, without saving anything.
- **Moving one downloaded song into the main library** by hand, rather than all or none through the General setting.

## Ideas for a later version (owner, 2026-10-01; brainstorming, nothing decided)

- **An artist's concerts.** Show upcoming shows (date, city, venue, a tickets link), as YouTube does above its results: on the YouTube search page, on an artist's page, or beside a song. Where it goes is to be decided; the function is what's wanted.
  - YouTube Music's data, as the engine reads it, has no events. The listings come from services such as Bandsintown and Ticketmaster, which have their own free APIs. Each needs a key (kept in the app's config folder, never in the repo), and the app needs to know roughly where the owner is (a "my city" setting) to show the nearest show first.
- *Built 2026-10-01 (see `docs/CHANGELOG.md`):* **The video that goes with a song**, in the app's own player, on the Local Visualizer page (a Cover / Video switch, lyrics alongside), with a quality menu like YouTube's. The owner wants the best quality that can be had (2026-10-01).
  - **Tested 2026-10-01 on one official music video, on the iMac:**
    - YouTube offered it in H.264 at 144p to 1080p, always as picture and sound in two separate streams (there was no combined version, even at 360p). 1440p and 4K exist only in VP9 and AV1, which Apple's player can't decode on this Mac.
    - Apple's player joined the 1080p picture stream and the format-140 sound stream into one item (an `AVMutableComposition` of the two remote assets). It was ready with its first frame in 0.2 to 0.3 s, played on, and a jump to 1:30 landed there. Being one item, picture and sound can't drift apart.
    - Each stream alone claims to be twice the video's real length (the same quirk as audio-only playback); the joined item, cut to the length YouTube gives, reports the right length.
    - Not checked: listening to it (the test ran silent; the player reported the sound track playing), long playback, a slow connection, or how often YouTube Music links a song to its video.
  - So: **possible up to 1080p with the app's own player and no new software.** Above 1080p would need a different video engine bundled with the app, which is a separate decision.
  - A song on YouTube Music and its music video are usually two different videos, often of different lengths. When the lengths differ, timed lyrics can't follow the video, so they'd be shown untimed.
- **Downloading the video (MP4).** yt-dlp can do it. Higher qualities need the picture and the sound joined into one file, which is a repackage, not a conversion. Files are large: roughly 50 to 200 MB a song.
  - **Owner, 2026-10-01: a saved video shows under Discover → Downloads. If it's moved into the library, it shows under Library → Videos.**
  - **Owner, 2026-10-01, later: rule 6 may be changed for this, and the size saved is the one chosen in the player.** Not built yet; what's still to settle is in `docs/KNOWN-ISSUES.md` → "Needs the owner's decision".
- **Discover → Lyrics** (owner, 2026-10-01): type a few remembered words of a song, get the songs they could be from, and play each one from YouTube Music.
  - **Tested 2026-10-01 with a four-word phrase from a song in the owner's library: neither source the app has can do it.** YouTube Music's search and LRCLIB's search both matched the phrase against song *titles* (many songs are called exactly that) and neither returned the song the words came from. So the earlier note that YouTube Music matches typed lyrics was wrong, at least for a phrase that is also a common title.
  - **What would work:**
    1. *Songs already in the library:* search the lyrics the library already holds (tags and `.lrc` files). No network, no key; the engine would keep a small index of the words.
    2. *Songs not in the library:* a service that searches the text of lyrics (Genius and Musixmatch both do), then look each answer up on YouTube Music by title and artist to play or download it. Needs a free API key from the owner, kept in the app's config folder (Settings → Accounts), never in the repo, and the service's terms checked first.
- **256 kbps can't be tested:** the owner has no YouTube Premium (2026-10-01), so that choice stays marked "coming".
